Nambli independent WARN audit — 2026-10-08

This read-only audit examined the final stock Fontspector 1.8.0 package
report, the corresponding check implementations, all twelve 1.000 font
files and the separate 1.001 candidate reports. No fonts were modified.

Not every warning is fixed or irrelevant. The final approved package has
151 WARN subresults across 12 check IDs. The original package had 152 across
13 IDs; adding the article images removed its one article/images warning.
These counts do not represent 151 distinct defective glyphs.

Exact final groups (sum 151)
48 alt_caron: manual review required by the structural check.
16 metadata/consistent_with_fonts: non-RIBBI legacy family naming.
 1 metadata/designer_profiles: designer catalog profile is absent.
12 metadata/unreachable_subsetting: real serving coverage work remains.
12 vendor_id: NAMB is not in the vendor registration lookup.
12 vertical_metrics: 1.6 UPM exceeds the recommended line-height range.
12 math_signs_width: proportional widths differ from the common math width.
 6 outline_colinear_vectors: near-collinear straight-segment joins.
 4 outline_jaggy_segments: sharp/reversing joins in approved 1.000.
 4 overlapping_path_segments: tiny overlaps in approved 1.000.
12 rupee: optional repertoire extension for this non-Indic family.
12 unreachable_glyphs: empty, unencoded .null at glyph ID 1.

Findings

All 48 alt_caron messages are decomposed-outline notices: the check cannot
inspect component identity in a simple glyph and requests manual review.
It has not diagnosed 48 incorrect accent shapes. The four relevant accented
letters per style are already shown in the visual review controls.

The 16 family-name warnings cover two platform records for eight non-RIBBI
faces. Keeping weight-specific legacy name ID 1 values and a common preferred
family follows current OpenType guidance. Related OpenType naming/grouping
checks pass. Forcing all ID 1 values to Nambli merely to silence this warning
could damage the four-style legacy grouping.
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/spec/name

The six collinear WARNs contain 47 joins. The checker uses a 0.1-radian
tolerance between consecutive straight segments; none of the 47 triples is
exactly collinear. Replacing each pair with one segment would move its middle
point from the outline by 0.006564–1.238336 font units. No mathematically
lossless deletion was identified. A visually negligible simplification may
be possible, but would need a separately validated outline candidate.

Every unreachable-glyph warning concerns only .null: no contour, advance 0,
GID 1, and no Unicode mapping. OpenType 1.7 recommended this legacy ordering;
current recommendations no longer list the old first-four-glyph convention.
Removal is possible modern-font cleanup, but would renumber later glyphs and
change the approved glyph count from 998 to 997. Retention is optional, not
a current mandatory requirement, and has no relation to the 16 tofu FAILs.
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/otspec170/recom
https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/spec/recom

The rupee check emits FAIL when an Indic detection character is present and
WARN for other fonts without U+20B9. Nambli takes the non-Indic WARN path.
Adding an original rupee design is an optional coverage extension.

Unreachable subsetting remains a real integration issue. Vendor registration
and the designer profile are administrative work. Line-height and math-width
warnings concern layout choices; changes require layout/clipping checks and
should not be described as repairs to corrupted glyphs.

Candidate boundary

Approved 1.000 binary-only stock: 134 WARN. Separate 1.001 binary candidate:
128 WARN. The only warning-count changes are jaggy_segments 4→2 and
overlapping_path_segments 4→0. All other groups remain unchanged. These six
removals are not in the Google submission or published release, and are not
deducted from the measured 151-WARN full-package result. A hypothetical
145-WARN full-package result has not been measured and is not claimed.
